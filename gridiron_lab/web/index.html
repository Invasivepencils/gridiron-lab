Play impact adds up changes in the team’s chance of winning on plays credited to this player. Expected points measures scoring value without weighting the game’s stakes. These are shared play credits, not what would have happened with a replacement.

Compare units separately as well as together. Passers, ball carriers and finishers are visible in these records; blocking, off-ball coverage, and many special-teams contributions are not. This is not a complete ranking of every player’s value.

What do these numbers tell me?

Understand play impact and the weekly story

nflverse’s win probability model estimates how each play changed the possession team’s chance of winning. Gridiron shares that change between named contributors: ordinary passes split equally between passer and target; interceptions stay with the passer; runs go to the carrier; lost fumbles go to the named fumbler. Named defensive finishers share the opposite swing. Kicks and returns go to the named kicker, punter or returner. No-play penalties, kneels and spikes are excluded. Credits on each team sum to at most one play’s swing.

Play credit is contextual and sensitive to these allocation rules. It is not value above replacement, causal credit, or a counterfactual replay. Season totals can exceed 100 points because they sum many games. Large negative plays count too. Expected-points credit uses the same allocation rules.

Understand team dependence and uncertainty

The comparison uses roster and snap evidence: at least 20% of the player’s primary unit’s snaps versus less than 20% (1% for kickers, punters and long snappers). At least three regularly involved and two limited games are required. Unknown participation is excluded; an injury report is displayed when present. A limited game is not automatically an injury.

A regularized full-season score-margin model adjusts team/opponent strength and home field using a design matrix and proper normal priors. A separate Bayesian two-group regression estimates each player’s involvement association, with a zero-centered 7-point effect prior and 12-point game noise. The interval is conditional on that fitted baseline; it excludes baseline uncertainty and teammate confounding. It cannot isolate a player’s causal contribution. Win-rate intervals use Beta(1,1) priors, with ties excluded from win rates and retained in score margins. Dependence sorting uses the lower model interval, not the biggest noisy point estimate.

Players who rarely miss games may have insufficient comparison evidence. That is not evidence they are unimportant. Multiple injuries, roster quality, opponent schedule, and playing time chosen in response to game state remain confounders.
